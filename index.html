Her work navigates between big and small data, the intimate scale of lived data, working at the intersection of qualitative and quantitative research, seeking to make complex territorial dynamics legible and understandable at different scales. In that legibility, she finds a bridge between design, research, and data-driven decision-making: a tool that informs and moves toward action, in service of communities and territories resisting unequal power dynamics.

The Ebro Delta is a unique, beautiful, and extremely fragile landscape, considered a climate change ground zero, meaning it undergoes drastic changes in short periods of time. In 15 years, 1.5 km of coastline disappeared. The photographs taken are an attempt to document this ephemeral landscape, given its vulnerability to changes in temperature and river flow, while also showing the enduring human footprint. As geologist Zalasiewicz (2014) stated, cities will be one of humanity's longest-lasting traces on the planet.
